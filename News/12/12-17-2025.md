### Private Markets – Key Developments

- **Private funds are actively managing risk in AI-driven data-center investments**  
  According to *FundFire*, private equity and private credit managers investing in data centers are increasingly avoiding speculative “build-first” projects amid concerns of an AI-related overbuild. Capital is instead being deployed where projects are supported by **long-term contracted revenues (typically 10–15-year leases)** with hyperscalers or large enterprise customers. This reflects tighter underwriting standards and a growing focus on cash-flow certainty to mitigate potential valuation and capacity risks.

- **Non-bank financial institutions now hold the majority of global financial assets**  
  Data from the **Financial Stability Board (FSB)**, reported by the *Financial Times*, show that non-bank financial institutions — including private credit funds, hedge funds, insurers, and asset managers — now control approximately **$257 trillion**, or **~51% of global financial assets**, surpassing traditional banks (~$191 trillion). Asset growth in the non-bank sector has been nearly **twice as fast as banking** in recent years. Regulators are increasingly concerned about leverage, liquidity mismatches, and opacity, and are signaling greater supervisory attention to private markets.

- **IPI Partners sanctions settlement underscores rising compliance risk for private funds**  
  **IPI Partners**, a U.S.-based private equity firm focused on data-center infrastructure, agreed to pay approximately **$11.5 million** to the U.S. Treasury’s **Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC)** to settle **51 apparent violations** of U.S. sanctions linked to Russian-related interests. The case highlights that sanctions compliance requires ongoing monitoring beyond deal execution. For private market managers, regulatory enforcement is becoming a **material financial and reputational risk**, particularly for funds with global investors and complex ownership structures.
