Fitch:  US private credit default rate (PCDR) eased slightly to 5.2% (TTM to Oct 2025) from 5.4% in September, with both its Privately Monitored (PMR) and Model-based (MCO) components ticking down (PMR default rate 7.7%, MCO 4.3%).
- Defaults are heavily skewed to smaller borrowers: issuers with ≤$25m EBITDA default at 10.9%, versus 2.9% for $25–50m EBITDA, reflecting weaker ability to absorb higher costs and shocks.
- Sector-wise, consumer products now shows the highest default rate among major sectors at 8.8% (amid tariffs, rising costs, weaker sentiment), while healthcare providers have the most defaulters (10) and a 6.2% default rate, with Medicaid-heavy providers seen as most vulnerable.

The Bank of England is launching a new “system-wide exploratory scenario” stress test focused on the fast-growing private credit market, in rseponse to the growing concerned about the rapidly expanding private credit market after the collapse of First Brands and  Tricolor 
- Blackstone, Apollo and KKR have agreed to participate
- The exercise will model how stress might spill over to banks, insurers, pension funds and the broader economy.
- if the test shows stress channels into banks, insurers and pensions, regulators may respond via higher capital, liquidity expectations, or concentration limits on exposures to private credit funds.
