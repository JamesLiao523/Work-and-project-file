### A leaked draft of the European Commission’s SFDR 2.0 proposal (ahead of its expected November 19 2025 release) outlines a complete overhaul of the existing EU sustainable finance framework.
Key Structural Changes
- Abolition of Article 8 and 9 categories

Replaced by three new product categories:
i) Transition investments
ii) Integration of sustainability factors
iii) Sustainability-related objectives

Each product must meet ≥ 70% alignment, mandatory exclusions, and a defined list of permitted investment types.

#### Removal of Principal Adverse Impact (PAI) reporting

- Scrapped at both entity and product levels.

- Reflects industry complaints about excessive reporting burden and unclear metrics.

#### Revised naming and marketing restrictions

- New rules on sustainability-related terminology to prevent greenwashing.

- Aligns more closely with UK’s SDR labels, but questions remain on potential hierarchies between categories.

#### Risk disclosures

- Largely unchanged, maintaining focus on transparency.

#### Scope reduction

- Portfolio managers and financial advisers appear to be excluded from mandatory product-level reporting.

### Big tech firms are turning to non-traditional financing (private credit, off‐balance sheet structures, vendor financing) to raise massive capital for AI/data infrastructure.
AI-leading companies are increasingly funding their data-center and infrastructure build-out through **private credit**, **off-balance-sheet structures**, and **vendor financing** instead of traditional bank loans.


#### Private credit

- **Blue Owl**
  - Acts as a **private-credit / private-capital provider**.
  - Finances Meta’s **$27bn Hyperion AI data center** via funds backed by pensions, insurers, etc.
  - Issues **private debt** (e.g., to Pimco and other institutional investors) to help fund its commitment.

- **Hyperion JV (Meta x Blue Owl) – funding side**
  - From the capital-source view, Meta is relying on **private-credit capital** (Blue Owl funds) instead of bank loans or public bonds.


#### Off-balance-sheet structures

- **Meta (Hyperion JV)**
  - Holds a **20% minority stake** in the Hyperion joint venture.
  - Records this as a **non-marketable equity investment**.
  - **Project debt sits in the SPV/JV**, so it is **not consolidated on Meta’s balance sheet** → classic **off-balance-sheet structure**.

- **xAI SPV (Nvidia investment)**
  - Nvidia invests **$2bn into a special-purpose vehicle (SPV)** tied to xAI.
  - Uses an SPV wrapper rather than simple on-balance-sheet bilateral lending.


#### Vendor financing

- **Nvidia (supplier/vendor side)**
  - Provides **capital + commercial guarantees** to its customers:
    - **Equity stakes** in customers (e.g., ~6.6% in **CoreWeave**, plus investments in **OpenAI** and **xAI**).
    - **Capacity / purchase commitments**, such as agreeing to buy up to **$6.3bn of CoreWeave’s unsold cloud capacity**.
    - **Chip-for-equity–style arrangements**, e.g., agreeing to buy 6 GW of AMD chips in exchange for **AMD stock warrants**.
  - This is effectively **vendor financing**: helping customers fund and scale infra so they can buy more Nvidia GPUs.

- **CoreWeave**
  - Receives **Nvidia equity investment** plus **long-term demand commitment** for its cloud capacity.
  - Uses this backing to **fund a capital-intensive neocloud business** and expand data centers.

- **OpenAI / xAI / other neoclouds (e.g., Fluidstack)**
  - Benefit from **Nvidia’s investment, chip commitments, and ecosystem support**.
  - Often combine **vendor financing** (from Nvidia) with **private capital** (from funds and institutional investors).

- **TeraWulf & Cipher Mining**
  - Ex-crypto miners that **pivoted to AI data-center hosting**.
  - Sign **long-term hosting/compute contracts** (e.g., 10-year agreements with Fluidstack), backed by vendor and private-capital ecosystems.
