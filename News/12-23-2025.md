Regulators Clear Path for Banks to Compete Again with Direct Lenders

* Regulators including the (OCC) and the (FDIC) have withdrawn the 2013 leveraged-lending guidance that constrained banks from underwriting high-leverage corporate loans.

* The guidance used a fixed debt/EBITDA threshold (often 6x) to trigger heightened scrutiny. That constraint is being replaced by broader, risk-based lending principles.

* Banks may regain share in direct corporate lending, leveraged buyouts, and similar credit markets where private credit funds have grown rapidly.
  
* This potentially erodes some of the competitive advantage private credit providers had

-----

Hedge funds are benefiting at the margin because private equity isn’t returning cash, investors now value liquidity more, and hedge fund returns have improved enough to look attractive versus private credit and PE.
