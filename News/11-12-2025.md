
### Hedge Funds Embrace Crypto as US Regulatory Environment Improves:
- In AIMA (Alternative Investment Management Association) global survey of 122 hedge funds (H1-2025), 55% reported crypto exposure; allocations are typically small <2% of AUM. 
- A majority (~67%) use derivatives (futures/options/swaps) rather than spot, enabling tactical positioning and easier risk limits without token custody.
- U.S. policy tone and the push for clearer, crypto-friendly rules are cited as key catalysts




### Private credit is facing a confidence squeeze: 

- Public markets reopened → huge refi wave: With HY/SL pricing tight (strong CLO demand), borrowers are refinancing out of pricier direct-lending deals, eroding private-credit managers’ pricing power
- Valuation signal: Listed BDCs trade below NAV —Cliffwater’s headline ~10.8% yield paired with an ~8% price discount implies ~9.9% yield on price, reflecting expectations of softer cash income.
- BDC's cash generation softens:  PIK interest and quiet (out-of-court) restructurings are up; base rates and all-in loan yields on new originations are slipping, while public markets remain cheaper (HY ~6.7%, BSL ~7.4%, private ~9.5%).

- Refis pull stronger credits out; what remains pays more in PIK and less in cash, so dividends and valuations are pressured until reinvestment economics improve or public-market demand normalizes.



### Australia’s ASIC review of 28 private-credit funds:

- Market snapshot: 8 wholesale non-registered and 20 retail registered funds; AUMs $11m–$12bn (avg $26.8bn); retail funds averaged 3,421 investors vs 378 for wholesale.
- Core deficiencies: Unclear reporting/terms, opaque interest margins/fees, weak governance & conflicts, poor valuation practices, and inadequate risk controls (poor stress prep).
- ASIC has already issued interim stop orders (e.g., La Trobe, RELI) and is actively scrutinizing disclosures and distribution controls.
2026 priority program: Make retail/advised distribution of private-credit funds a top focus—tightening rules/expectations on fee & margin transparency, independent valuation/provisioning, liquidity design, and the role of research houses in ratings and due diligence.
