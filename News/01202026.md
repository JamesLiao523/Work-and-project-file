Private Credit: What’s Actually Happening (Straightforward 5 Bullets)

Redemptions and risk signals are real
Investors pulled $7 billion+ from major private credit funds late 2025 as concerns about credit quality increased after high-profile corporate bankruptcies — a rare but meaningful investor reaction. Funds reported redemption spikes around ~5 % of assets.

Credit quality stress is rising
Senior loan writedowns have tripled since 2022, and distressed loans with heavy markdowns are increasing, suggesting more borrowers struggle with higher rates and leverage.

Despite warnings, fundraising keeps growing
Even as defaults tick up and some critics warn of structural fragility, private credit managers continue to raise large amounts of capital globally — including record and jumbo funds — showing strong investor demand still exists (notwithstanding selective risk aversion). [Reflecting the theme of the CNBC article about warnings dismissed]

Industry pushback on fear narratives
Large managers like BlackRock argue that rising defaults are a normal cyclical correction, not systemic collapse, and highlight continued robust fundraising — including over $220 billion raised in 2025 — to support that narrative.

Liquidity and structural risk remain key watch points
Semi-liquid and evergreen vehicles, valuation opacity, and covenant erosion are structural stress markers that critics highlight — meaning liquidity mismatches and “hidden” credit risk could surface under broader economic stress. JPMorgan forms new advisory group to tap private markets boom


JPMorgan created a new advisory team called Private Capital Advisory & Solutions to help companies and sponsors raise capital in private markets.

The team aims to deepen JPMorgan’s role in private equity and private capital fundraising as alternatives continue to grow.

It will combine private capital advisory functions with M&A expertise to offer a broader suite of services.

The group is led by Keith Canton, reporting to senior advisory and capital markets leadership.

The move reflects JPMorgan’s strategy to stay central in deal origination and advisory as companies stay private longer and private financing expands.
